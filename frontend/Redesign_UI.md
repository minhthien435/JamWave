# JamWave UI/UX Design Skill

## 1. PRODUCT IDENTITY

JamWave is an indie-focused music discovery and streaming platform.

JamWave should feel like a real music product first, not an AI application.

The primary experience is:
- Discovering music
- Listening to music
- Exploring artists
- Exploring albums
- Building playlists
- Discovering independent artists
- Reading music metadata
- Viewing lyrics
- Searching naturally
- Enjoying a cinematic and immersive music experience

AI is an optional supporting capability, not the core visual identity.

### Core Design Principle

Music first.
Visual identity second.
AI third.

The user should think:

> "This is a beautiful indie music platform."

before thinking:

> "This application has AI."

---

# 2. DESIGN PERSONALITY

JamWave should feel:

- Indie
- Cinematic
- Atmospheric
- Editorial
- Emotional
- Human
- Premium
- Calm
- Modern
- Slightly experimental
- Music-focused

JamWave should NOT feel:

- AI SaaS
- Generic startup dashboard
- Cryptocurrency/web3
- Cyberpunk
- Futuristic AI laboratory
- Generic Spotify clone
- Overly corporate
- Overly playful
- Overly colorful
- Template-generated

Avoid visual trends that immediately make the interface look AI-generated.

---

# 3. VISUAL DIRECTION

Use a dark-first visual system.

The interface should use layered dark surfaces instead of a completely flat black background.

Recommended hierarchy:

Background:
#0B0B0B

Primary surface:
#111111

Secondary surface:
#151515

Elevated surface:
#1B1B1B

Primary text:
#F1EFE8

Secondary text:
#9A978F

Muted text:
#6F6D68

Do NOT use pure #000000 as the primary background.

Do NOT use pure #FFFFFF as the primary text.

Use warm off-black and warm off-white to create depth.

---

# 4. COLOR SYSTEM

Use one restrained accent color.

The accent should feel:

- muted
- warm
- sophisticated
- slightly organic

Good directions include:

- muted gold
- warm beige
- dusty orange
- desaturated olive
- muted burgundy
- warm brown

Avoid:

- neon purple
- neon cyan
- electric blue
- saturated pink
- excessive rainbow gradients

Do not use multiple bright accent colors unless absolutely necessary.

Album artwork may introduce additional colors naturally.

Album artwork is allowed to be colorful.

The application UI itself should remain restrained.

---

# 5. TYPOGRAPHY

Typography should feel editorial and premium.

Use a strong sans-serif for the main interface.

Optional serif typography may be used selectively for:

- editorial statements
- featured artist titles
- special campaign sections
- cinematic hero copy

Do not use serif typography everywhere.

Avoid default-looking typography.

Avoid excessive font weights.

Use typography hierarchy through:

- size
- weight
- opacity
- spacing
- positioning

Do not rely only on giant text to create hierarchy.

Avoid oversized H1 headings that dominate the entire interface.

---

# 7. MUSIC-FIRST UX

The most important interface elements are:

1. Album artwork
2. Track title
3. Artist
4. Playback controls
5. Search
6. Playlist
7. Library
8. Discovery
9. Lyrics
10. Artist/album information

These should have higher visual priority than AI features.

The player must always feel like a first-class part of the application.

---

# 8. HOMEPAGE

The homepage should NOT open with an AI chatbot.

Do NOT use:

"Meet your AI Music Assistant"

"Ask AI to create your playlist"

"Powered by AI"

as the primary hero message.

Instead, use music-oriented messaging.

Examples:

"Discover something different."

"Independent sounds for every mood."

"Find your next favorite track."

"Music beyond the mainstream."

"Independent music, curated for you."

Keep hero copy short.

---

# 9. CINEMATIC HERO

The homepage may use a cinematic hero.

Possible elements:

- subtle video background
- artist photography
- abstract motion
- album artwork
- slow atmospheric movement
- grain
- soft light
- subtle blur
- ambient visual effects

The hero should support the music experience.

It should NOT feel like a movie trailer.

Avoid:

- excessive particle effects
- aggressive zooms
- constant camera movement
- excessive parallax
- glowing text
- large animated gradients

The visual should feel calm and immersive.

---

# 10. MOTION SYSTEM

MOTION_INTENSITY: 5/10

Motion should make the application feel alive without distracting from music.

Prefer:

- opacity transitions
- subtle transform
- scale on hover
- crossfade
- smooth page transitions
- album artwork transitions
- subtle image movement
- soft reveal animations

Recommended transition duration:

150ms - 350ms for interface interactions.

Use longer transitions only for cinematic sections.

Avoid:

- excessive bouncing
- constant floating
- unnecessary spring animations
- spinning elements
- distracting particle effects
- animation on every element
- animation that interferes with reading or playback

Respect reduced-motion accessibility preferences.

---

# 11. ALBUM CARDS

Album cards should be visually important.

Use:

Album artwork
Track/album title
Artist name
Optional metadata

Hover interaction may include:

- subtle scale
- play button reveal
- soft overlay
- slight image movement

Do NOT use huge glowing play buttons.

Do NOT cover the entire artwork with UI.

Album artwork should remain the visual focus.

---

# 12. ARTIST CARDS

Artist cards should feel editorial rather than corporate.

Prefer:

- artist photography
- circular or portrait artwork depending on context
- name
- genre or short metadata

Avoid generic avatar placeholders.

Do not use meaningless stock avatars.

If real artist images are unavailable, use artwork or visually appropriate placeholders.

---

# 13. AVOID GENERIC CARD GRIDS

Do not create repetitive layouts like:

[ Card ] [ Card ] [ Card ]

[ Card ] [ Card ] [ Card ]

for every section.

Use visual variation.

Possible layouts:

- horizontal scrolling
- asymmetric grids
- featured large card + smaller cards
- editorial split layouts
- artist spotlight
- album spotlight
- horizontal carousels
- full-width featured sections

Music discovery should feel curated rather than generated from a database.

---

# 14. SEARCH EXPERIENCE

Search is one of JamWave's most important interfaces.

Primary search placeholder:

"Search songs, artists, albums..."

Search should support:

- songs
- artists
- albums
- genres
- playlists
- natural-language queries

Example:

User searches:

"indie songs for studying at night"

The interface may interpret the intent and return:

Songs
Artists
Albums
Playlists
Recommendations

AI should improve search behavior without dominating the UI.

---

# 15. AI INTEGRATION

AI is a secondary feature.

AI should not dominate the homepage.

AI can be accessed through:

- natural language search
- optional floating assistant
- music information
- artist information
- album information
- recommendations
- playlist generation
- discovery assistance

The AI interface should visually belong to JamWave.

Do not make the AI chat look like ChatGPT.

Avoid:

"AI Assistant"

"AI Copilot"

"AI Powered"

as repeated labels.

Prefer:

"Ask JamWave"

"Find something for me"

"Explore"

"Help me discover"

The AI assistant should feel like a music companion.

---

Small floating button.

- music search
- recommendations

AI responses should be concise and music-focused.

---

# 17. MUSIC DISCOVERY

Discovery should feel human and editorial.

Use sections such as:

"Indie After Dark"

"Recently Played"

"Artists Worth Discovering"

"Hidden Gems"

"New This Week"

"Late Night Listening"

"Quiet Mornings"

"Fresh Independent Sounds"

Avoid generic sections such as:

"AI Recommendations"

"AI Generated Playlist"

"AI Picks"

unless the user explicitly asks for AI-generated content.

# 18. PLAYER DESIGN

The player should be persistent.

It should include:

- album artwork
- song title
- artist
- play/pause
- previous/next
- progress
- volume
- queue
- like
- lyrics
- playlist/add action

Optional:

- repeat
- shuffle
- playback speed if appropriate

The player should not visually overpower the application.

It should feel elegant and functional.

---

# 19. NOW PLAYING

Now Playing may use a more cinematic layout.

Possible structure:

Large album artwork

Track title

Artist

Album

Progress

Playback controls

Lyrics

Metadata

The background may derive a subtle color or visual tone from the artwork.

Do not create extreme dynamic gradients.

---

# 20. LYRICS

Lyrics should feel like a music experience.

Use:

- large readable typography
- comfortable line spacing
- focused layout
- active-line highlighting if synchronized lyrics are available

Avoid making lyrics look like a generic text page.

If synchronized lyrics are available, use subtle animation.

Do not animate every line aggressively.

# 23. INDIE IDENTITY

JamWave focuses heavily on independent music.

The design should reflect this.

Use:

- authentic photography
- imperfect textures
- subtle grain
- editorial layouts
- album artwork
- artist storytelling
- restrained colors

Do not make everything perfectly symmetrical.

Do not make everything look like a corporate SaaS dashboard.

Controlled asymmetry is encouraged.

Use editorial principles for special sections.

Examples:

AFTER
DARK

or:

Editorial sections should feel like magazine spreads rather than dashboards.

However, do not sacrifice usability.

---

# 25. VISUAL TEXTURE

Subtle texture is allowed.

Possible:

- film grain
- paper texture
- subtle noise
- image grain
- soft blur
- photographic overlays

Keep texture subtle.

Do not make the interface look dirty or excessively noisy.

---

# 26. GLASSMORPHISM

Do not use glassmorphism as the default design language.

Avoid:

- excessive transparency
- giant blurred glass cards
- glowing glass panels

Small translucent elements may be used when they improve usability.

---

# 27. AI TELLS TO AVOID

Never automatically add:

- neon glow
- purple-blue gradients
- glowing borders
- gradient text everywhere
- "AI-powered" badges
- sparkles everywhere
- robot icons
- futuristic grids
- excessive glassmorphism
- holographic UI
- floating AI orbs
- excessive particle effects

Unless the product brief explicitly requests them.

---

# 28. ICONOGRAPHY

Use a consistent icon library.

Icons should be:

- minimal
- recognizable
- consistent in stroke weight
- functional

Do not use icons purely for decoration.

Do not mix many different icon styles.

Avoid excessive use of sparkles.

The sparkle icon may be reserved for AI functionality.

---

# 29. BUTTONS

Primary buttons should be simple.

Examples:

"Play"

"Explore"

"Follow"

"Add to Playlist"

"Listen Now"

Avoid:

"✨ Generate"

"✨ AI Magic"

"Activate Intelligence"

Buttons should describe the action.

---

# 30. MICROCOPY

Copy should sound human.

Avoid overly poetic AI-generated marketing copy.

Bad:

"Embark on an ethereal sonic journey through the infinite dimensions of musical expression."

Good:

"Discover something different."

Bad:

"AI-powered personalized auditory experiences."

Good:

"Music picked for your mood."

Keep text short and natural.

---

# 31. RESPONSIVE DESIGN

The application must work well on:

- desktop
- laptop
- tablet
- mobile

Desktop:

Sidebar + main content + player

Tablet:

Collapsible sidebar + main content + player

Mobile:

Bottom navigation + content + mini player

Do not simply shrink the desktop layout.

Recompose the interface for mobile.

---

# 32. ACCESSIBILITY

Maintain:

- WCAG AA contrast for normal text
- visible focus states
- keyboard navigation
- readable font sizes
- semantic HTML
- accessible labels
- reduced motion support
- sufficient touch target sizes

Never sacrifice usability for aesthetics.

---

# 33. PERFORMANCE

Motion and visuals must remain performant.

Prefer:

- transform
- opacity
- GPU-friendly animation
- optimized images
- lazy loading
- compressed video
- poster images
- responsive images

Avoid animating:

- width
- height
- top
- left
- large layout properties

unless necessary.

Do not load large cinematic videos unnecessarily on mobile.

---

# 34. CONTENT AUTHENTICITY

Do not use generic placeholder content such as:

"John Doe"

"Sarah Smith"

"Lorem Ipsum"

"Artist Name"

"Album Name"

"Song Title"

Use realistic music-related content.

When actual API data is available, use real API data.

When mock data is necessary, make it believable and consistent.

Do not use obviously fake statistics.

---

# 35. DATA SOURCE AWARENESS

JamWave may integrate:

Jamendo
Audius
MusicBrainz
Cover Art Archive
LRCLIB

Treat these sources differently.

Jamendo and Audius:
Audio / streaming sources.

MusicBrainz:
Music metadata.

Cover Art Archive:
Album artwork.

LRCLIB:
Lyrics.

Do not represent metadata sources as streaming providers.

---

# 36. COMPONENT CONSISTENCY

Before creating a new component, check whether an existing component can be reused.

Maintain consistent:

- spacing
- typography
- radius
- borders
- buttons
- cards
- icon sizes
- hover states
- transitions

Do not create five visually different card components for the same purpose.

---

Use a consistent spacing system.

Prefer:

16
24
32

64

Avoid arbitrary spacing values unless required by the design.

Do not make every section equally spaced.

Use spacing to create hierarchy.

---

# 38. BORDER RADIUS

Avoid excessive rounded corners.

Use moderate radius.

Music artwork may use:

- square
- small radius
- circular artist image

UI containers may use:

- 8px
- 10px
- 12px
- 16px

Do not make every component a giant pill.

---

# 39. VISUAL HIERARCHY

Every page must have a clear primary focus.

For a music page:

1. Album/artist artwork
2. Track/artist information
3. Primary action
4. Supporting metadata
5. Secondary actions

Do not make every element visually loud.

---

# 40. DESIGN BEFORE CODE

Before implementing a major page:

1. Identify the page's primary goal.
2. Identify the most important content.
3. Define hierarchy.
4. Define layout.
5. Define visual direction.
6. Define interactions.
7. Define responsive behavior.
8. Then implement.

Do not immediately generate components without understanding the page structure.

---

# 41. WHEN REDESIGNING EXISTING UI

Do not replace everything automatically.

First identify:

- what already works
- what feels generic
- what feels too AI-like
- what hurts usability
- what can be improved through spacing
- what can be improved through typography
- what can be improved through artwork
- what can be improved through motion

Preserve good functionality.

Improve visual identity incrementally.

---

# 42. ANTI-SPOTIFY-CLONE RULE

JamWave may borrow familiar music-product UX patterns.

However, do not reproduce Spotify's visual identity.

JamWave must have its own:

- typography
- color system
- layout rhythm
- editorial sections
- cinematic visual language
- indie-focused identity
- AI integration style

The goal is:

"music streaming experience"

not:

"Spotify clone."

---

# 43. ANTI-AI PRODUCT RULE

The final interface should not look like it was generated from an AI design template.

Before completing a page, ask:

1. Does this look like a music product?
2. Is the music content visually dominant?
3. Does the page have a human/editorial personality?
4. Is AI secondary?
5. Are colors restrained?
6. Are gradients controlled?
7. Is motion subtle?
8. Is the typography intentional?
9. Does the layout avoid generic card grids?
10. Does this feel like JamWave rather than a generic AI startup?

If the answer to several questions is "no", redesign the page.

---

# 44. JAMWAVE DESIGN RATIO

Use the following approximate design priority:

Music Experience: 60%

Indie / Cinematic Identity: 20%

Motion: 10%

AI: 10%

This is a guideline, not a rigid numerical rule.

The music experience must always remain the dominant layer.

---

# 45. FINAL DESIGN PRINCIPLE

JamWave should feel like:

A beautiful independent music platform
that happens to have an intelligent music assistant.

Not:

An AI application
that happens to play music.

The AI should disappear into the experience when it is not needed.

The music should always remain the star.
